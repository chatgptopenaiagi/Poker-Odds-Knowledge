# HIL cash-style rules v1.0.0

HIL supports 2–6 seats, one local human, local heuristic bots, integer play chips, no rake, antes, straddles, rebuy during a hand, or multiple boards. A hand uses a standard 52-card deck and one board. The strongest five cards out of seven win; zero, one, or both private cards may play. Suits never break ties.

The button rotates to the next funded seat. Busted seats sit out. At the transition from multiway to heads-up, the button is adjusted if needed so the surviving previous big blind does not post the big blind twice in a row. Heads-up the button posts the small blind, acts first before the flop, and last after the flop. With three or more funded seats, blinds sit left of the button and preflop action begins left of the big blind. Postflop action begins with the first eligible player left of the button. Each street requires every nonfolded player with chips to act and match the current wager, unless the hand ends first. Blind posting is not an action and does not remove the big blind's option.

All displayed raise sizes mean **raise TO**, the player's total contribution on this street. The amount removed from the stack is TO minus the player's existing street contribution. The opening minimum bet is the big blind. After a wager, the minimum raise adds the last full valid bet/raise increment. An all-in may be smaller; a non-all-in may not. A sub-minimum all-in opening wager of 5 with a minimum bet of 10 may be called for 5; an unacted player's minimum raise is TO 15. This no-limit game does not use limit-poker completion rules.

The short opening rule was independently checked against [the Poker TDA-hosted no-limit versus limit clarification](https://www.pokertda.com/forum/index.php?topic=842.0) (reply 6, inspected 2026-09-30). Its no-limit example adds a full minimum increment to the short all-in amount; completion only up to the ordinary minimum belongs to its contrasting limit example. [Robert's Rules, section 14, No-Limit rule 2](https://www.keywestresortcasino.com/roberts-rules-of-poker.html) publishes the same no-limit example. HIL tests both rejecting a non-all-in completion to 10 over 5 and accepting a full raise to 15, plus reopening a prior checker when cumulative short openers reach 10.

An actor records both whether they have acted and the wager level which reopens their raising rights. At their action, that level is the wager faced plus the last full valid increment. A single short all-in does not reopen action. Multiple short all-ins can reopen it if, when action returns, the accumulated increase reaches that actor's full increment. Calling an intervening short raise establishes a new threshold for that actor. A prior checker facing less than a minimum bet cannot raise. An unacted player retains the right to make a full raise. This interpretation follows the big-bet reopening principle and cumulative short-raise examples in [Poker TDA rules, rule 49](https://www.pokertda.com/view-poker-tda-rules/) (inspected 2026-09-30); HIL adopts these betting mechanics for its explicitly defined cash-style learning rules rather than claiming a full tournament implementation.

A short big blind does not reduce the nominal preflop bring-in while two other players can still wager. Once only one nonfolded player has chips, that player may only resolve an outstanding call/fold against the actual all-in wager; they cannot bet against nobody. If they owe nothing, or all remaining players are all-in, the board runs out automatically. The engine burns one card before each board street. Simulation-only burns and unseen deck cards are not known dead cards in learner analysis.

At round end, any contribution above the second-highest street contribution is returned to its owner as an uncalled wager. The engine records returns separately. Pots are contribution layers: all contributed money is included, while folded players are ineligible to win. Each pot is evaluated separately among its eligible players. Tied shares use integer division; leftover odd chips go one at a time to tied winners clockwise starting left of the button. This rule is deterministic, independent of suits and card order. If everyone but one player folds, that player receives all contested contributions; no board or hole-card showdown is required.

The pure reducer validates legal actions and expected revisions, copies its input, and never consults timers. `createHand` retains exact initial stacks, blinds, button and full deck in private local history; `act` appends versioned actions; `replayHand` reconstructs any action boundary and verifies the log. A branch is a new working copy. It does not mutate the saved original. A session ends cleanly when fewer than two seats remain funded; a confirmed training reset can provide new play chips.

Normal deals use `crypto.getRandomValues` and unbiased bounded-integer Fisher–Yates. Deterministic fixtures use the separately documented seeded generator in the card module. Policy and equity sampling receive separate random streams. Local history necessarily contains the completed deal; HIL does not claim adversarial secrecy or regulatory certification.

Bots receive only their own two cards and copied public state/actions. They never receive the full engine state, deck, burns, other hole cards, or hero analysis. Optional bounded equity comes from the same observation. Their fallback score is explicitly a policy heuristic, not showdown equity; profiles are not GTO, Nash-optimal, professional-strength, or commercial-solver substitutes.
